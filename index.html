User guide

      
    

    


      

      
Install on the home screen

      
Do this once: it then runs offline, looks like a normal app, and saving works properly.

      
    
        
  1. Open this page in Safari — Chrome cannot add to the home screen.
    2. 
        
  2. Tap Share → Add to Home Screen → Add.
    3. 
        
  3. Launch from the new icon. Try it once in airplane mode.
    4. 
      

      
Opening the PDF straight from the Files app puts iPadOS into a preview mode
      where saving and the share sheet may not work — the home screen icon avoids that.


      
Quick start

      
    
        
  1. Load the flight plan PDF. The ICAO flight plan appears under the box,
            reassembled across page breaks; Copy gives it as one line.
    2. 
        
  2. Document fields — ATIS, ATC CLRNC, altimeter settings, PIC BLOCK. Free text
            uses the space around the dots, with a counter for what's left. PIC BLOCK shows the
            planned block fuel greyed alongside, for reference only — never written into the
            document.
    3. 
        
  3. Takeoff time, UTC, four digits — everything else follows from it.
    4. 
        
  4. Actuals — ATO and remaining fuel per waypoint. Enter moves to the next field.
    5. 
        
  5. Altimeter cross-checks — one row per hour of flight.
    6. 
        
  6. Save PDF — Save to Files, AirDrop, Print, or ForeFlight.
    7. 
        
  7. Weather & NOTAMs and Charts — read straight out of the loaded
            package.
    8. 
      

      
The ETD button fills in the off-block time from the plan — takeoff is
      normally later, so check it.


      
Numeric keypad

      
Every digits-only box opens the app's own keypad instead of Safari's —
      docked to the bottom and built to match the system's, key for key. The corner button
      plays whatever that field's own Return key would have: Next across a run of
      boxes, Go on the takeoff time, Done wherever nothing moves.

      
It dismisses the way a system keyboard does, too: the iPad's own dismiss key, pinned to
      the keypad's own bottom-right corner, always just closes it, and so does a tap outside it
      or a real scroll.

      
Mirrored opposite it, top left, Previous steps back the same run
      of boxes — greyed out on the first one, since there is nowhere behind it to go.


      
Altimeter cross-checks

      
Company rule: an hourly comparison. The app finds the waypoint on each full hour after
      takeoff and prints your reading on the line under it. A row turns amber as it nears, red
      once overdue, with a short tone the first time. Saving with checks missing asks for
      confirmation.


      
Fuel checks

      
Company rule: a fuel check on overflying a waypoint, or at least every 30 minutes. The
      next due time shows above the table — amber as it nears, red once overdue — and the fuel
      box it belongs to is ringed. A figure entered anywhere inside that half hour clears it.
      Saving with a check overdue asks for confirmation.

      
A figure entered early restarts the thirty minutes from that moment, rather
      than leaving the next one due on the old half-hour mark.


      
Following the flight

      
The table tracks the clock: passed points fade back, the last one is shaded, and the
      one you're running to is highlighted and kept centred. It holds still while a box has
      focus, and for twenty seconds after you scroll by hand. The highlight follows your
      ATO entries rather than the clock, so it stays right even when actuals run a few
      points behind.


      
Direct to a waypoint

      
Press Direct to… and tap the waypoint you're cleared to. Skipped waypoints stay
      where they are, struck through and faded — the order still has to match the paper form —
      and the target is marked DCT. Nothing reaches the document and no ETO is rewritten;
      only the highlight moves. The toolbar chip undoes it, and each direct remembers just the
      waypoints it cut out.


      
Weather and NOTAMs

      
Read entirely out of the loaded package — no network, no account, no key. The dropdown
      groups aerodromes as this flight, areas along the route and other
      aerodromes. Each gives METAR and TAF as printed, then NOTAMs with their number,
      validity and subject, then company NOTAMs. Reports are exactly as old as the document —
      re-brief from the current source before acting on them.

      
The TAF change group and any NOTAM the flight actually meets are picked out — departure
      at ETD, destination and alternates at ETA, the en-route alternate at the flight's midpoint.
      A plan carrying no date of flight gets no highlight.

      
A NOTAM about the runway is marked red, the taxiway amber — read off its own words,
      muted the way a fuel check already is, never a traffic light. Naming both marks it for the
      runway. The list itself never reorders.

      
The whole aerodrome closed outranks both: it gets the one saturated fill on the card,
      the same red as Reset, since it is worth catching before the rest of the list is
      even read.

      
Highlight what applies now, beside the aerodrome dropdown, turns all
      three off at once for a crew that would rather read the reports plain. It is a device
      preference, kept the same way the theme is — set once, left as it is after that.


      
Wind components and weather charts

      
Open charts pages through the wind components, tropopause/MORA profile and
      significant weather charts on their own. Arrow keys or Prev/Next move between them,
      Zoom toggles full size with scrolling, Escape closes.


      
Getting the plan in

      
Tap the box, or drag the PDF out of Files — put it alongside in Split View or Slide
      Over, press and hold the plan, and drop it on the box. On a computer it can also be
      pasted; not on iPad, since Safari never hands a PDF to a web page.


      
If iPadOS closes the app

      
The plan and everything entered are kept on the device, so opening the app again
      restores it — takeoff time, actuals, altimeter readings and which OFP it is. Checks
      already announced don't sound again. Reset clears it and returns to an empty drop
      zone.


      
Updates

      
The app updates itself — the icon never needs removing and re-adding. With no plan
      loaded it comes back on the new version on next launch; with one open it waits until you
      close it, so nothing moves while you're using it. Offline it never updates: the version
      you leave the ground with is the version you fly with.

Good to know

      
    
        
  • Only Air Astana computerized flight plans are supported.
    • 
        
  • Entries save automatically and come back when you reload the same plan; Reset clears
            them.
    • 
        
  • Nothing leaves the iPad and no connection is ever needed.
    • 
        
  • Route ID, request number and release time sit under the title in the header, so the
            plan on screen can be checked against the one you were given.
    • 
        
  • The sun and moon buttons switch the screen theme; print colours never change.
    • 
        
  • A warning about ET and T/T means the plan's own columns disagree — check those
            waypoints by hand.
